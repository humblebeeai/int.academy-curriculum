# Contributors Guide

Help keep the HumbleBeeAI Open Curriculum sharp, current, and useful.

This curriculum is meant to be community-built and continuously improved. AI evolves fast. Roles change. Tooling changes. A static curriculum becomes outdated. Your contributions help reduce noise for learners worldwide and improve the quality of the pathway.

---

## Who should contribute

This guide is for experienced practitioners who can add signal, not noise.

You are a good fit if you have:
- 5+ years relevant experience (industry or research)
- the ability to curate open resources responsibly
- the ability to write clear learning outcomes, exercises, and project specs

If you are a learner, you can still help by:
- reporting broken links
- suggesting improvements to clarity
- proposing missing topics or better sequencing  
(implementation PRs should still meet the quality gates below)

---

## What you can contribute

In scope:
- module improvements and restructuring
- projects and case studies
- exercises and rubrics
- templates for evaluation and reporting
- track improvements and curriculum mapping
- translations (when enabled)

Out of scope:
- certification answer keys
- internal evaluation materials

---

## Contribution principles

**Outcome-first**  
State what the learner can do after finishing.

**Proof-first**  
Every module must produce proof (exercises or a project, ideally both).

**Role alignment**  
Map the contribution to Core Systems or a role track.

**Maintainability**  
Prefer clean structure and strong modules over long lists.

**Respect creators**  
Reference open resources correctly. Do not re-publish restricted content.

---

## Quality gates (required)

A contribution should satisfy all gates:

### Gate A - Credible authorship
- 5+ years relevant experience or equivalent research background.

### Gate B - Open and reusable sources
- Resources must be free and open.
- HumbleBeeAI-authored writing and structure are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- Third-party resources stay under their original licenses.

### Gate C - Exercises and projects included
- clear deliverables
- acceptance criteria
- suggested evaluation approach

### Gate D - Evaluation clarity
- a rubric or checklist
- common pitfalls and failure cases

---

## What “good” looks like

A strong module makes it easy for a learner to:
- understand the goal
- follow a coherent set of resources
- produce outputs that can be inspected
- evaluate results correctly
- avoid common mistakes

If your contribution does not produce proof, it is not done.

---

## Submission template (recommended)

Copy and fill this template for any new module:

### Module title
- Short, specific, searchable.

### Role mapping
- Core Systems or one of: Data Science, CV, NLP/LLM, GenAI, AI Software Engineering.

### Prerequisites
- What the learner must already know.

### Learning outcomes (measurable)
Write outcomes as capabilities, not topics.  
Examples:
- “Implement a baseline and a comparison model with correct splits.”
- “Run an ablation and write a short conclusion based on evidence.”

### Open resources
- List links plus 1 line each: why this resource is chosen.

### Exercises (expected outputs)
- What the learner must submit or produce.
- Include acceptance criteria.

### Project spec (deliverables and acceptance criteria)
- required deliverables (repo, README, report, results)
- run instructions (setup and execution)
- evaluation criteria (metrics, baselines, error analysis expectations)

### Evaluation rubric or checklist
- pass/fail checks
- what “excellent” looks like
- typical mistakes

### Time estimate (rough hours)
- Give a rough estimate for focused study.

### Common pitfalls
- List the most likely ways learners fail or misinterpret results.

Suggested workflow:
1. Post a short proposal (outcomes, role mapping, resources).
2. Align scope in discussion.
3. Submit a full module using the template.
4. Review by designated maintainers and department heads.
5. Merge and publish with version notes.

---

## PR checklist (before you open a PR)

- [ ] Role mapping is clear (Core Systems or a track)
- [ ] Outcomes are measurable (capabilities, not topics)
- [ ] Sources are free/open and properly referenced
- [ ] Exercises and/or project exist with deliverables
- [ ] Evaluation rubric/checklist included
- [ ] Common pitfalls included
- [ ] No restricted content is copied into the repo
- [ ] The module is structured for maintainability

---

## Governance

Final approvals are handled by HumbleBeeAI department heads and core maintainers, with academic oversight where relevant. The goal is consistent standards and a coherent pathway.
